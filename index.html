I lead the creative function end-to-end: setting the visual standard, directing the work, developing the designers who produce it, and building the systems that let quality scale without adding headcount. In practice that has meant three things at once — raising the ceiling on output quality, raising the floor on team capability, and shortening the distance between a brief and an approved asset.

Defined a single, defensible standard for what leaves the agency — applied across branding, campaigns, social and digital, in both Arabic and English.

How the team was trained

Diagnose before teaching

Reviewed each designer's live output and identified the specific bottleneck — ideation, execution speed, technical finish or consistency. Training was targeted, not generic.

Demonstrate on real client work

No sandbox exercises. Every technique was introduced on a live brief the designer already owned, so the value was immediate and the learning stuck.

Define where AI is allowed

Set explicit boundaries — where generative output is production-ready, where it is reference only, and where it is prohibited. This is what prevented an increase in speed from becoming a drop in quality.

Review against a written standard

Feedback tied to a documented quality bar rather than personal preference, so designers could self-check before submitting and improve between reviews.

Transfer ownership

Progressively reduced supervision as capability was proven. The objective was never dependence on me — it was designers who hold the standard themselves.

Workflow Systems / 08

Systems, so quality does not depend on who is on shift.

Individually strong designers produce inconsistent output without a system around them. These are the systems introduced during the review window — plus the improvements currently in discussion with management.

System 01

Structured approval path

A defined route from brief to release with named owners at each gate. Work is challenged internally before it reaches the client, and nothing ships without a recorded approval.

  • Fewer client-side revision rounds
  • Errors caught at the cheapest point in the process
  • Clear accountability when a deadline is at risk
System 02

Written creative standard

The quality bar is documented — typography, hierarchy, bilingual treatment, image direction, brand compliance. Designers self-check against it; reviews became shorter and less subjective.

  • Consistency across designers and accounts
  • Faster onboarding for any future hire
  • Feedback that is objective and defensible to the client
System 03

Campaign template systems

Each account has a reusable typographic and layout system rather than one-off designs. New posts extend an existing system instead of restarting the design decision.

  • Higher throughput at the same headcount
  • Brand consistency enforced by structure, not vigilance
  • Junior designers can produce senior-standard output
System 04

Cross-team collaboration protocol

Defined handover points with content, performance and development, so creative arrives with the context it needs and campaign objectives are visible to the designer producing the asset.

  • Fewer briefs returned for missing information
  • Creative aligned to performance objectives from the start
  • Less senior time spent chasing clarification
