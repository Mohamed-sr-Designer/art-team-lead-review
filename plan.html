60-Day Action Plan

Following my performance evaluation as Art Team Lead. This document does not defend the score. It identifies the root causes, sets out eight initiatives with owners and measures, and commits to a checkable result in 60 days.

Evaluation received 4/10 Accepted without qualification. The score reflects a real gap between what the role requires and how I worked in my first 90 days.
Target at day 60 7/10 Measured against the success criteria in this document, scored by the General Manager, not by me.
Plan structure 8 initiatives Three critical, three high, two medium. Eight weeks, weekly deliverables, and two formal checkpoints.
Executive Summary / 01

What went wrong, and what changes.

I optimised for output when the role required me to build capability. The work improved. The team did not become independent. The second one matters more to the business, and I got the balance wrong.

In my first 90 days I treated every quality problem as something to solve myself. That was the fastest route to a good result, and in month one it was probably the right call. I never switched off that mode when the reason for it expired. The outcome is the pattern named in my evaluation: a lead who produces instead of develops, a team that waits for my answer, and a single point of failure sitting in one person.

The same instinct explains the other feedback. I introduced four significant changes inside 90 days without an adoption plan, because I could already use them myself. I let one to one meetings slip because delivery felt more urgent than alignment. I absorbed overload with my own hours instead of raising it as a business decision. None of this is a team problem. Each one is a leadership decision I made.

This plan is built to be checked rather than believed. Every initiative has a named owner, a date and a measure. Where I do not have a baseline, I say so and set it in Week 1 before claiming any improvement. Two checkpoints are requested with the General Manager: day 30 and day 60.

Root Cause / 02

Three decisions, not seven problems.

The evaluation lists seven areas. They are not seven separate failures. They trace back to three decisions I made, and fixing the decisions fixes most of the list.

Cause 01

I chose output over capability

Output is visible in week one. Capability is not visible for months. I took the measurable path and produced the work myself, which is why the quality moved and the team did not.

Explains: Leadership, Team Dependency, Solo Player, and the uneven team development.
Cause 02

I ran change by volume, not by sequence

Four changes in 90 days, each announced rather than co-designed. No adoption ladder, no champions, and no clear answer to what any of it does for the person being asked to change.

Explains: Change Management, and the resistance I initially read as a team attitude problem.
Cause 03

I had no capacity model, so I became the release valve

With no view of committed work against available days, every request looked urgent. When capacity ran out I extended my own hours instead of escalating a scope decision. That hid the problem instead of solving it.

Explains: Prioritisation, Time Management, and the missed one to one meetings.

Action Plan / 04

Eight initiatives, in priority order.

Open any initiative for the full detail: current gap, business impact, root cause, action steps, timeline, owner, success metrics, risk, mitigation and expected outcome. The three critical items all address team dependency and my own behaviour, because nothing else works until those move.

60-Day Roadmap / 05

Eight weeks, eight deliverables.

Each week produces something written that can be checked. Week 1 deliberately produces no improvement, only measurement, because improvement claimed without a baseline is not a result.

Week 1Day 1 to 7

Measure before changing anything

Baseline
  • Record the starting number for every KPI in this plan. Nothing is described as improved until there is a number to improve from.
  • Book every one to one and the weekly planning session for the full 8 weeks as fixed calendar blocks.
  • Draft the decision rights matrix: what the team decides alone, with a peer, or with me.
  • Run an anonymous five question team survey so the assessment is not only mine.
  • Written note to the team taking responsibility and setting out what changes in how I work.
DeliverableBaseline sheet agreed with the GM. Calendar locked for 8 weeks. Decision matrix v1. Survey results shared unedited.
Week 2Day 8 to 14

Hand over ownership

Ownership
  • Assign a named owner to every account, including the client conversation, not only the artwork.
  • The no first drafts rule starts on client work. Every exception is logged with a written reason.
  • Decision rights matrix reviewed and signed off with the GM.
  • First full round of one to ones completed, with written notes shared inside 24 hours.
DeliverableAccount ownership map. Exception log opened. All one to one notes shared.
Week 3Day 15 to 21

Write down the standard

Documentation
  • Publish the creative standard as a one page checklist a designer can apply without me.
  • Score the skills matrix for all five people jointly with the GM.
  • First knowledge sharing session, led by a team member rather than by me.
  • Draft one development plan per person, each with a single target skill for the 60 days.
DeliverableCreative standard v1. Scored skills matrix for five people. First session delivered by a team member.
Week 4Day 22 to 30

Reset the change agenda

Change management
  • Confirm the freeze on new initiatives with the GM. One change selected to finish properly: the AI production workflow.
  • Co-design the adoption steps with the two people furthest from it.
  • Name two champions and hand them the next sessions.
  • Day 30 checkpoint with the GM, read against the Week 1 baseline.
DeliverableAdoption ladder published. Two champions named. Day 30 review held and minuted.
Week 5Day 31 to 37

Delegate the decisions

Delegation
  • Decision rights matrix goes live. Escalations to me are counted from this point.
  • Work in progress limits set per designer.
  • Weekly triage with account management running: what ships, what moves, what is reduced.
  • Capacity view live: committed work against available design days.
DeliverableEscalation log started. Capacity view in use. First scope escalation raised to the GM through the proper route.
Week 6Day 38 to 44

Test the independence

Stress test
  • Three consecutive days where I review nothing and approve nothing. The team runs its own releases.
  • Everything that breaks is recorded as a gap in the system, not as a fault in a person.
  • A fix is assigned to each gap found, with an owner and a date.
DeliverableLead absent test report shared with the GM, listing what failed and the fix for each item.
Week 7Day 45 to 51

Balance the growth

Team development
  • Review coaching hours per person and rebalance deliberately toward whoever received least.
  • Mahmoud delivers a peer coaching session on one named skill.
  • Shared brief template agreed with content and performance.
  • Designers attend their own campaign kick offs instead of me relaying.
DeliverableCoaching time report showing the gap closing. Peer session delivered. Brief template agreed across teams.
Week 8Day 52 to 60

Prove it, then hand it over

Evidence
  • Full KPI read against the Week 1 baseline, including anything that did not move.
  • Repeat the anonymous team survey and compare it with Week 1.
  • The team presents its own work to the GM. I do not present it for them.
  • Day 60 review against the success criteria, and the next 60 days proposed from the data.
DeliverableKPI comparison sheet. Second survey result. Team led presentation. Signed day 60 review.

Risk Assessment / 07

What could stop this working.

Each risk is real and most of them are caused by the plan itself. A plan that only lists upside is not a plan.

Quality dips while ownership transfersHigh

Designers taking full ownership for the first time will release work that I would have caught. On a live client account that is a real commercial risk, not a training exercise.

Mitigation: Handover is staged at one account per designer per week. Every first release goes through a peer check against the written standard. I remain accountable to the client for the outcome throughout.
Delivery pressure pulls me back into producingHigh

The first genuinely tight deadline will make taking the file back look like the responsible choice. This is exactly how the pattern formed the first time.

Mitigation: The exception log makes every relapse visible to the GM in the same week it happens. When a deadline is at risk the route is a scope escalation, not a private decision to produce it myself.
The team does not want the responsibilityMedium

Ownership is being handed to people who did not ask for it, in the same period they are being asked to adopt a new workflow. That is a lot at once, and it is the same mistake I made with change volume.

Mitigation: Ownership is discussed in the one to one before it is assigned, not announced. Anyone can decline a specific account and say why, and that answer becomes data about what is missing rather than a performance issue.
One to ones become status meetingsMedium

The easiest failure mode. The meeting happens, the box is ticked, and nothing about development actually changes.

Mitigation: The agenda puts development before status. If the session runs short, status is cut. The team survey in Week 8 asks directly whether the sessions were useful, and I do not get to write that answer.
Behaviour changes but the perception does notMedium

Leadership behaviour is largely invisible from outside the team. It is possible to do all of this and still be read the same way at day 60.

Mitigation: Weekly written update to the GM, two formal checkpoints, and a day 60 review presented by the team rather than by me. The evidence is designed to be observed by someone other than me.
The initiative freeze is read as retreatLow

Pausing new initiatives could look like stepping back from the improvements I introduced, or like a loss of ambition.

Mitigation: The freeze is about sequencing, not value. The backlog stays visible and dated, so the GM can see exactly what is queued for the next cycle and decide if the order is wrong.
Blind Spots / 08

Things that were not raised, but should have been.

These were not in the evaluation. I am adding them because leaving them out would make the plan look better than the situation is.

My own 90-day review is evidence for the criticism

I built that document alone, and one of its sections presents work I produced personally as a leadership achievement. Read against this evaluation, it is a clear artefact of the solo player pattern rather than an argument against it.

Fix: At the day 60 review the team presents its own accounts. I will also add a section to that document written and owned by each designer about the account they now run.

"He understands my creative thinking" is still dependency

I described Mahmoud absorbing my judgement as the clearest success of the quarter. It is not the same as a transferable standard. If the standard lives in two heads instead of one, the risk has halved but it has not been removed.

Fix: The standard is written down in Week 3 and then tested on someone who has not absorbed my taste. If a new hire can apply it without me, it is a standard. If not, it is still preference.

I presented overtime as commitment

In my own review I described working late as the cost of moving fast. Read correctly it is evidence of the prioritisation and delegation gaps in this evaluation. A workload only I can carry is not a strength, it is a design fault in the role.

Fix: My weekly hours become a reported metric. Overload becomes a scope escalation with options attached, decided by the GM, rather than a private extension of my day.

I added three people to a team that had not absorbed the last change

The three hires were the right call for capacity, but the timing increased the coaching load at the exact point where my coaching capacity was already the constraint. More people against an undocumented standard makes the dependency worse, not better.

Fix: The three new hires are onboarded against the written standard and owned by a senior designer, not by me. Their time to first unassisted release becomes a measure of whether the documentation actually works.

Every assessment of my team is my own

I have scored the team, described their growth and judged the resistance, all from my own view. I have never asked them what is unclear, what blocks them, or what they need from me that they are not getting. That is a significant gap in a leadership evaluation.

Fix: An anonymous five question survey in Week 1 and again in Week 8, with results shared with the GM unedited, including anything that reflects badly on me.

Executive Commitment · 10

I accept the evaluation. The score reflects a real gap between what this role requires and how I worked in my first 90 days. I optimised for output because output was the thing I could control and prove quickly. That produced good work and a team that is not yet independent, and the second one matters more to this business.

This plan is written to be checked, not to be believed. Every commitment in it carries a date, an owner and a measure. Where there is no baseline, I have said so and I will set it before claiming any improvement.

I am asking for two things: the day 30 and day 60 checkpoints held in the calendar, and a decision from you when scope escalations arrive, so that overload becomes a business decision rather than my personal overtime.

If the numbers do not move by day 60, that is also a result. I will bring it to you with the same detail as if they had.
