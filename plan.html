Part 1 / 01

Gaps with the team, and the solutions.

Six initiatives that move the function from lead led to team owned. Open any one for the current situation, the business impact, the action plan, the risks and mitigation, the timeline and the success metrics.

Part 2 / 02

Time consuming.

Lead capacity is the scarcest resource in the function. This is how it is allocated today, where it moves over the 60 days, and the five reallocations that release it.

Current allocationToday
  • Final review across the full account portfolio
  • Direct production on high stakes briefs
  • Cross functional meetings attended on the team's behalf
  • Ad hoc problem solving as requests arrive
  • Overflow capacity absorbed at lead level
  • One to ones and planning, scheduled around delivery
Target allocation at day 60Day 60
  • Coaching and one to ones, scheduled first and protected
  • Exception review only: new accounts, new designers, high risk work
  • Weekly planning and capacity management
  • Standard setting, documentation and quality governance
  • Client direction, pitches and new business
  • Overflow raised as a scope decision rather than absorbed

Review coverage

01

Final review currently runs across every account. It is the single largest consumer of lead capacity and the point where throughput is capped.

Moves to: exception based review. Named owners plus a written standard plus a peer check handle routine work. Lead review is reserved for new accounts, new designers and high risk output.
Measured by: share of work released under owner authority

Direct production

02

Producing directly protects quality on the day, but it converts lead hours into a single deliverable rather than into capability that compounds.

Moves to: paired direction. The designer holds the file and I direct. First drafts stay with the designer from Week 2, with exceptions documented.
Measured by: lead hours on client first drafts

Cross functional representation

03

Attending content, performance and development meetings on the team's behalf keeps campaign context at lead level and adds a relay step to every brief.

Moves to: the account owner attends their own kick offs. Lead attendance is reserved for meetings where the client relationship requires it.
Measured by: cross team meetings attended by designers

Ad hoc problem solving

04

Resolving a blocker directly takes minutes and reads as efficient. It transfers the fix without transferring the method, so the same question returns.

Moves to: a coaching response. Understand the reasoning first, then direct. Slower once, and it removes the recurring cost.
Measured by: repeat escalations on the same issue

Overflow capacity

05

When committed work exceeds available days, absorbing it at lead level keeps delivery intact but removes the signal that capacity has been exceeded.

Moves to: a weekly capacity view and a scope escalation with options attached: reduce scope, move the date, or add capacity. The decision sits with the GM.
Measured by: scope escalations raised, and lead hours per week

Alignment time

06

One to ones and planning have been scheduled around delivery, which means alignment happens during execution rather than ahead of it.

Moves to: protected blocks booked for the full 60 days in Week 1, ahead of delivery commitments rather than after them.
Measured by: one to one completion rate within the scheduled week

6

Medium

How I will set priorities for the team

Priorities are currently set per request, which makes everything look urgent. From Week 2 they are set per week, using the same four steps, so capacity decisions are made with data rather than in the moment.

Step 01See the capacityList the work we promised against the design days we actually have that week.
Step 02Ask three questionsIs the date real? What breaks if it moves? Can we cut scope instead of the date?
Step 03Set a limitEach designer works on a set number of items at a time. Nothing new starts until something finishes.
Step 04Escalate, do not absorbOver capacity goes to you as a choice: cut scope, move the date, or add people. Not into my evenings.

Part 4 / 04

A plan for each person, not one plan for everyone.

My team is not at one level, so one plan for all of them does not work. I split them by how ready each person is to own work today. This is how initiative 04 gets executed: capability spread across the team rather than concentrated in a few people.

How I grouped them: by how ready each person is to own an account and carry the decisions that come with it today. It is not about talent or effort, and it is not permanent. We score it together in Week 3, and I expect people to move from Group B to Group A inside the 60 days. That movement is one of the things we measure.
Group A · Ready to own

Turn them into owners

Goal: people who own projects and need very little supervision from me.

60-Day Roadmap / 05

Eight weeks, eight things you can check.

Week 1 deliberately delivers measurement rather than improvement. An improvement claimed without a baseline cannot be verified, and an unverifiable plan is not worth reviewing.

Week 1Day 1 to 7

Establish the baseline

Baseline
  • Write down the starting number for everything in the table above.
  • Book every one to one and the weekly planning meeting for eight weeks. These do not move.
  • Draft the list of who decides what: alone, with a colleague, or with me.
  • Run a short anonymous team survey, so the view is not only mine.
  • Tell the team, in writing, what I am changing about how I work.
You getThe starting numbers, agreed with you. A locked calendar. The decision list, first version. The survey result, unedited.
Week 2Day 8 to 14

Hand over the accounts

Ownership
  • Give every account a named owner, including the client conversation.
  • The no first drafts rule starts. Every exception is written down.
  • Review and agree the decision list with you.
  • First full round of one to ones, with written notes sent inside 24 hours.
  • Weekly priority meeting starts with account management.
You getThe account owner list. The exception log, open. All one to one notes.
Week 3Day 15 to 21

Write down the standard

Documentation
  • Publish the quality rules on one page a designer can apply independently.
  • Score the skills sheet for all five people, together with you.
  • First sharing session, led by a team member, not by me.
  • Draft one growth plan per person, one skill each.
You getThe quality rules, one page. The scored skills sheet for five people. The first team led session.
Week 4Day 22 to 30

Fix how I bring change

Change
  • Agree the stop on new ideas with you. One change to finish: the AI workflow.
  • Build the steps with the two people who like it least.
  • Name two people from the team to lead the next sessions.
  • Day 30 check with you, read against the Week 1 numbers.
You getThree simple levels of use, published. Two named leads from the team. The day 30 review, written down.
Week 5Day 31 to 37

Let the team decide

Delegation
  • The decision list goes live. I start counting what still comes to me.
  • Set a limit on how many items each designer works on at once.
  • The capacity view goes live: promised work against available days.
  • First over capacity case goes to you as a choice, not into my evening.
You getThe count of what still comes to me. The capacity view. The first scope decision, raised the right way.
Week 6Day 38 to 44

Run the handover exercise

Test
  • Three days where the team approves and releases its own work.
  • Log every issue that surfaces. These are gaps in the system, not in the people.
  • Each issue gets a fix, an owner and a date.
You getThe handover exercise report, including every issue found and how each one is closed.
Week 7Day 45 to 51

Spread the growth

People
  • Check my coaching hours per person and give more time to whoever got least.
  • Mahmoud teaches one skill to the rest of the team.
  • Agree one shared brief template with content and performance.
  • Designers go to their own kick off meetings instead of me.
You getThe coaching hours report, showing the gap closing. The peer session. The agreed brief template.
Week 8Day 52 to 60

Show the result

Proof
  • Read every number against Week 1, including anything that did not move.
  • Run the same team survey again and compare it with Week 1.
  • The team shows its own work to you. I do not present for them.
  • Day 60 review against the success list, and I propose the next 60 days from the numbers.
You getThe before and after numbers. The second survey. A team led presentation. The signed day 60 review.

My commitment · 07

I accept the review. The score shows a real gap between what this job needs and how I worked in my first 90 days. I worked on the output because output was the thing I could control and show quickly. That gave good work and a team that is not independent yet, and the second one matters more to this company.

This plan is written so you can check it, not so you can believe it. Every promise has a date, an owner and a number. Where there is no starting number, I have said so, and I will measure before I claim anything.

I am asking you for two things: keep the day 30 and day 60 checks in the calendar, and give me a decision when I bring you a scope choice, so that too much work becomes a business decision instead of my own overtime.

If the numbers do not move by day 60, that is also a result. I will bring it to you with the same detail as if they had.
