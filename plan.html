60-Day Action Plan

This is my plan after the review. I am not arguing with the score. The page has four parts: the gaps between me and my team and how I fix them, where my time goes wrong, what I do first, and a development plan for each person in the team.

My review score 4/10 I accept it. There is a real gap between what this job needs and how I worked in my first 90 days.
Target at day 60 7/10 Measured against the success list at the end of this page, scored by you, not by me.
The plan 6 gaps Each one has a solution, a timeline, a way to measure it, and a priority.
Summary / 01

What went wrong, in one line.

I worked on the output when my job was to build the people. The work got better. The team did not become independent. The second one matters more.

In my first 90 days I fixed every quality problem myself. It was the fastest way to a good result, and in the first month it was probably right. I never stopped doing it when the reason went away. That is where the feedback comes from: a lead who produces instead of teaches, a team that waits for my answer, and one person the work depends on.

The same habit explains the rest. I brought in four big changes in 90 days without a plan for how people would learn them, because I could already use them myself. I let my one to one meetings slip because delivery felt more urgent. I took extra work into my own evenings instead of raising it as a business decision. None of this is the team's fault. Each one is a choice I made.

This page is written so you can check it. Every solution has a date, a number and a priority. Where I do not have a starting number yet, I say so, and I will measure it in Week 1 before I claim anything improved.

Part 2 / 04

Where my time goes.

My hours are high and the team is not growing. That means my time is going to the wrong work. Here is where it goes today, where it should go, and the five things that eat it.

My week todayNow
  • Reviewing every piece of work before it goes out
  • Making designs myself when quality is at risk
  • Sitting in every meeting with content, performance and development
  • Solving problems people bring me, in the moment
  • Working late to finish what did not fit in the day
  • One to one meetings, when there is time left
My week at day 60Target
  • Coaching and one to one meetings, booked first, not last
  • Reviewing only new work, risky work and new people
  • Planning the week and protecting the team from overload
  • Teaching the standard so people can check their own work
  • Client and pitch work, where a lead is actually needed
  • No late nights. If we are over capacity, it comes to you as a decision

I review everything

Drain 01

Every item comes to me before it goes to the client. I am the last gate on all work, on all 12 accounts.

Fix: Each account gets an owner. The quality rules go on one page so people can check their own work. A colleague checks first. Only new or risky work comes to me.

I make the work myself

Drain 02

When quality is at risk I take the file. It is the fastest fix today and it costs me the most hours.

Fix: No first drafts by me from Week 2. The designer keeps the file and I coach. If a date is at risk we cut scope, I do not take the work back.

I am the messenger between teams

Drain 03

I go to the meetings with content, performance and development, then pass the brief on. The context stops with me.

Fix: The account owner goes to the kick off instead of me. I go only when the client relationship needs it.

I solve instead of teach

Drain 04

Someone is stuck, I fix it in two minutes. It feels efficient. The same question comes back next week.

Fix: Three questions before I answer. It takes ten minutes today and saves the same question every week after.

I absorb the extra work at night

Drain 05

When there is more work than days, I work late. That hides the problem instead of showing it to anyone.

Fix: A simple capacity view each week. When we are over capacity it comes to you with options: cut scope, move the date, or add people.

One to ones go last

Drain 06

They are booked, then delivery takes the slot. This is why my manager says I focus on execution and not on alignment.

Fix: All one to ones booked for eight weeks in Week 1. These blocks do not move for delivery work.

How I will set priorities for the team

Prioritisation was in my review as well. Today we decide per request, so everything looks urgent. From Week 2 we decide per week, with the same four steps every time.

Step 01See the capacityList the work we promised against the design days we actually have that week.
Step 02Ask three questionsIs the date real? What breaks if it moves? Can we cut scope instead of the date?
Step 03Set a limitEach designer works on a set number of items at a time. Nothing new starts until something finishes.
Step 04Escalate, do not absorbOver capacity goes to you as a choice: cut scope, move the date, or add people. Not into my evenings.

Part 4 / 06

A plan for each person, not one plan for everyone.

My team is not at one level, so one plan for all of them does not work. I split them by how ready each person is to own work today. This answers gap 04 directly: growth spread across the team instead of concentrated in one person.

How I grouped them: by how ready each person is to own an account and make decisions without me today. It is not about talent or effort, and it is not permanent. We score it together in Week 3, and I expect people to move from Group B to Group A inside the 60 days. That movement is one of the things we measure.

Blind Spots / 08

Things that were not said, but should have been.

These were not in my review. I am adding them because leaving them out would make this plan look better than the real situation.

My own 90-day review proves the point against me

I built that page alone. One whole section shows work I made myself and calls it a leadership result. Read next to this review, it is proof of the solo player habit, not proof against it.

Fix: At the day 60 review the team presents its own accounts. I will also add a part to that page written by each designer about the account they now own.

"He understands my thinking" is still a dependency

I called Mahmoud learning my taste the best result of the quarter. It is not the same as a written standard. If the standard sits in two heads instead of one, the risk is smaller but it is still there.

Fix: The standard goes on paper in Week 3, then we test it on someone who has not learned my taste. If a new person can use it without me, it is a standard. If not, it is still my opinion.

I showed overtime as commitment

In my own review I described late nights as the price of moving fast. Read properly, it is proof of the prioritisation and delegation gaps in this evaluation. Work that only I can carry is not a strength. It is a fault in how I built the role.

Fix: My weekly hours become a number I report. Over capacity becomes a choice for you, with options, instead of a quiet extension of my day.

I hired three people into a system nobody had learned yet

The three hires were right for capacity, but the timing added more coaching work at the exact moment my coaching time was already the problem. More people with no written standard makes the dependency worse, not better.

Fix: The three new people are trained against the written standard and owned by a senior designer, not by me. How fast they reach their first solo release tells us if the documents actually work.

Every opinion about my team is my own

I scored the team, described their growth and judged the resistance, all from my side only. I never asked them what is unclear, what blocks them, or what they need from me and are not getting. In a leadership review, that is a big gap.

Fix: A short anonymous survey in Week 1 and again in Week 8. You get the results as they are, including anything that looks bad for me.
